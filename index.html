01 — The part nobody warns you about

Be honest. Is any of this you too?

We asked students what the most annoying part of dealing with their furniture really was. Here's what kept coming back — and the numbers behind it.

01
Something to move — but no car, no van, no one to help?
Sound familiar?
  • I had a free couch and nobody could come and pick it up.
  • Renting a van just to shift one wardrobe across the city wasn't worth it.
  • Getting anything bigger than a box from one side of Maastricht to the other was the whole battle.
The item itself is rarely the problem. Getting it from your door to someone else's is. So it ends up on the curb instead.
90% named arranging transport as their single biggest hurdle when furnishing a room.
"With big items, you don't want to rent an expensive van before you even know whether the thing is worth it. The transport, and being sure about the condition — that's what would make the difference for me." From a student interview
02
Tried to sell it — and drowned in "is this still available?"
Sound familiar?
  • Endless messages asking if it's available, then total silence.
  • People haggling for an hour over a five-euro chair.
  • Buyers who promise to come and then simply vanish.
When selling turns into a part-time job, most people give up. The effort outweighs the money, so good furniture gets given away — or binned.
87% agree that selling second-hand furniture is too much hassle for what you get out of it.
"I sold more than 1,500 euros' worth of things for around 300. Purely because of the time pressure, I lost hundreds on stuff that had nothing wrong with it." From a student interview
03
Old contract ends before the new room is ready?
Sound familiar?
  • My contract ended before my new place started, so I had nowhere to put anything.
  • It all had to happen on one single day, or not at all.
When the dates don't line up, there's no in-between. And the quickest thing to do with furniture you can't store is throw it out.
89% said a lack of time drove what they did with their furniture.
"If I'd been able to store my things the moment I had to move out, and then sell them calmly afterwards, my whole problem would have been solved." From a student interview
04
That uneasy feeling about meeting a stranger from a group chat?
Sound familiar?
  • Half the WhatsApp groups just feel sketchy.
  • You never really know whether a stranger will turn up — or pay.
Deals between total strangers come with no guarantee and no accountability. So people fall back on dumping things — it feels safer than getting burned.
92% knew of channels where they could have sold or given things away — and still didn't use them.
"The most important thing is trust. I want to be sure the other person is a real student, and that my money is only gone once the items are actually there." From a student interview
